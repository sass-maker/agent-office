# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

The initial user is an owner operating a very small organization from a Mac.
They want useful non-technical AI employees without managing prompts,
workflows, or agent infrastructure.

Human employees may join the same organization later, but they are not part of
the POC.

## Product Purpose

Let an owner create an organization, hire named AI employees, give them
responsibilities and outcomes, and supervise their coordinated work through a
shared, persistent workplace.

The product gives agents a durable position between a person and a software
process. An employee is hired, addressed by name, trusted with a bounded scope,
and held accountable for outcomes; its execution remains inspectable,
permissioned, interruptible software.

The POC succeeds when a basic content team can pick up where it left off,
advance real local artifacts through a bounded writer-manager review loop, show
its blockers and next work, and stop cleanly at End Day.

## Positioning

The durable object is an employee rather than a chat or workflow: an identity
with a role, memory, workspace, responsibilities, skills, and eventually
scoped authority. Models, tools, and machines can change without replacing the
employee.

The product deliberately keeps five things separate: employee identity,
installed employee package, assigned skills, declared tools and granted
authority, and the model or runtime currently powering execution. Hiring an
employee creates a working contract; it does not silently grant a connection or
turn a package into an identity.

This is an employee and agent-management system, not an autonomous-company
system. Money, legal identity, ownership structures, corporate governance, and
unsupervised publishing are outside the POC. The hard problem here is giving
agents useful scopes of responsibility and giving the owner excellent ways to
direct, observe, interrupt, review, and retain their work.

## Commercial Model

The Mac workplace is intended to be a one-time paid application: the owner
buys and keeps the environment where their organization lives. AI employees
are separate employable products. Some may be included as a starter team;
others may be optional subscriptions because their creators continuously
operate models, tools, proprietary data, skills, or machines on the employee's
behalf.

The owner subscribes to people, not generic software seats. Each employee must
declare the permissions and recurring costs it requires, and the organization
may grant less authority when the employee supports a safe reduced mode.

## Operating Context

The owner opens a native Mac application, sees a cosy two-dimensional office,
checks goals, blockers, and a task board, starts the day, observes employees
working and reviewing one another, intervenes when asked, and ends the day.
State and artifacts remain in a local organization folder.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Initial specialists: an editorial manager, a researcher, a content writer,
  and a Customer Voice Analyst, coordinated through the owner's executive
  assistant.
- Initial work: a reusable outcome loop for any AI employee plus one seeded
  content/SEO workday, research notes, a draft, bounded review cycles, and a
  daily report.
- New organizations begin with installed employee packages and no silently
  hired team. Hiring materializes a durable employee identity and an editable,
  revisioned working contract.
- Each working contract records responsibilities, assigned skills, declared
  connection identifiers, explicit capability grants, execution provider and
  model, local environment, review policy, autonomy boundaries, and change
  provenance. It never contains credentials.
- Every AI employee has Communication as a built-in skill: they must leave
  attributable progress updates, delivery notes, and precise requests for help.
- The owner gives outcomes to employees, not directly to a generic task runner.
  Each employee has an independent, resumable queue and may decompose an
  approved outcome into one to four Mission tickets, delegate bounded tickets
  to qualified hired colleagues, and choose among existing skills.
- Plans may require owner approval; delivery and acceptance are distinct; the
  owner can reply in context, redirect, reprioritize, reorder, reassign, pause,
  stop, request bounded revisions, accept, or close work without erasing its
  history.
- Local execution is capacity-bounded across employees and serialized within
  each employee. One employee's cancellation or blocker does not stop another's
  independent outcome.
- An employee may not grant itself a skill, permission, integration, publishing
  right, spending authority, or broader contract.
- Initial execution stays local. No publishing or external system writes.
- Codex may power employee work through the user's existing local
  authentication; the POC must not embed API keys.
- A future employee may be human or AI and may receive skills, a separate
  machine, integrations, permissions, budgets, and guardrails.
- The product name is deliberately undecided.
- No dollar price, checkout, or employee marketplace is validated or live yet.

## Brand Commitments

- A beautiful, cosy, game-like workplace with the visual quality and legibility
  of AI Town as a reference—not an enterprise dashboard or HR suite.
- Human names and recognizable avatars.
- The opening surface prioritizes the living workplace, goals, blockers, and
  the task board.
- The visual world must make real work state legible rather than showing fake
  decorative activity.

## Evidence on Hand

- The product definition and first-slice decisions were established directly
  with the owner in the canonical project conversation.
- One early visual probe exists outside the repository; it is directional
  evidence, not an approved asset or implementation screenshot.
- No customer evidence, benchmarks, pricing, or public product claims exist.

## Product Principles

1. Build a usable small organization before building a general platform.
2. Employees are durable identities; models and machines are replaceable.
3. The workplace must reflect real tasks, artifacts, reviews, and blockers.
4. Capability and autonomy increase only alongside permissions and guardrails.
5. Local ownership and inspectability come before integrations.
6. Outcomes belong to employees; tickets are their plan, not a second work
   system.
7. Delivery is evidence for owner judgment, not automatic acceptance.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Core state and actions must remain available outside the 2D scene through
native text, controls, keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion behavior. The
same semantic interface roles support authored light and dark appearances;
compact navigation keeps its labels, candidate contracts disclose details
progressively, and compact Company membership becomes a vertical directory
instead of a horizontally scrolling organization chart.
