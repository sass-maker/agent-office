## Design Specificity Verdict

Highly product-specific and authored. The dark-spruce presentation case, real
Mac workplace, named employee folios, workday ledger, and ownership deed cannot
be relabeled for an unrelated SaaS product without rebuilding the composition.
The deterministic detector returned zero findings.

## Overall Impression

The page makes the product feel like a place someone owns rather than a
dashboard they rent. After revision, the application is legible in the first
desktop and mobile view, while the workday remains the clearest proof of how an
employee organization behaves.

## What's Working

- The real native application is the dominant proof rather than future claims.
- “Own the workplace. Subscribe to the people.” explains the commercial model
  without a generic pricing table.
- Maya, Nia, and Theo create the emotional peak while remaining concrete work
  roles with artifacts and handoffs.

## Priority Issues

No unresolved P0 or P1 issues remain after the hero, CTA, mobile navigation,
target-size, favicon, asset-loading, and contrast corrections.

- **P3:** Several derived material colors remain local CSS literals. Promote
  them only when another surface needs the same material roles.

## Persona Red Flags

- **First-time owner:** The revised primary path now demonstrates the workday;
  the terminal is explicitly secondary developer access.
- **Mobile visitor:** Explore navigation, 44-point targets, and first-viewport
  application proof are present without horizontal overflow.
- **Skeptical buyer:** POC status, absent checkout, and unvalidated pricing are
  stated directly rather than hidden in fine print.

## Minor Observations

- The page is intentionally long; a future real signup or purchase action may
  justify shortening the local-build passage.
- The working title is now labeled at the first brand encounter.

## Questions to Consider

- Which owner-facing action should replace the workday walkthrough once a real
  distribution channel exists?
- Should the eventual product name retain the feeling of a small inhabited
  place, or emphasize the employable-agent market?
